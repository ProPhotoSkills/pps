Pay attention to your surroundings

PSYCHO, T03_L04

Written by Eric P.

Development - your invisible next-level trigger →

Introduction

Who is actually standing behind you?

When you think about how you have grown as a photographer – or want to grow – sooner or later this one question comes up that we all like to ignore: who is actually standing behind you? Not in front of you, not next to you, but behind you. Invisible. Supportive. Sometimes challenging, sometimes annoying, sometimes life-saving. People are the foundation of every development, even if in the creative field we often like to stage ourselves as lone wolves. It is this image of the lone fighter who builds an entire world with a camera, vision and courage. Believe me: this image is romantic, but it is not true.

Behind every president, every famous personality, every icon, every creative force there have always been people who made their strength possible in the first place. Women, men, partners, teams, critics, mentors, quiet forces nobody sees but without whom everything would collapse. And in photography it is no different. On the contrary: in photography, your surroundings are not just a "nice to have". They are an absolute multiplier. They decide how far you get, how fast you learn, how you master crises, how you find inspiration and how you deal with pressure.

When you raise the camera, you are not just raising a tool. You are raising a piece of personality. And how stable that piece of personality is depends on how stable your surroundings are. Of course your own energy counts, your will, your discipline, your eye, your technique and your inner attitude. But all of that only unfolds if the environment in which you develop is right.

You certainly know this: there are people who move you forward and people who hold you back. There are voices that make you bigger and voices that make you smaller. And sometimes a single sentence, a single look, a single attitude is enough to make you doubt or grow. Your surroundings are like a permanent filter. They colour your perception, they influence your decisions, your creativity, your willingness to take risks. And without you noticing, they determine how you see yourself.

Some photographers are rebellious characters by nature. They become good despite their surroundings. But they carry traces too. And if you look more closely, you will find that even the greatest loners had someone at some point who supported them. Whether it was a partner who went through every night shift without complaint. A friend who accompanied the first shoots. A mentor who showed a way. A client who believed in them before they believed in themselves. Nobody grows in a vacuum. You don't either. Nobody makes the next-level development alone. And that is a good thing.

Because development is not a technical process. It is an emotional one. And emotions arise in relationships. When you learn a new technique, that is simple. When you test a new lighting setup, that is logical. But when you reach your limits, you need people who keep you stable. And that is exactly where it is decided how far you can go.

Your surroundings are like an amplifier for your photographic potential. They make sure your ideas have room. That your vision is not nipped in the bud. That you dare to make mistakes. That you keep developing instead of standing still. That you have backing when you take a new step. Supportive surroundings are like invisible insurance for your creative freedom.

Think of a big, demanding production: nobody does it alone. Not even the photographers who act as if they rock everything on their own. There are assistants, lighting technicians, stylists, make-up artists, producers, set runners, clients, models. And although all these people work in the background, they are largely responsible for whether an image works in the end or not. Now transfer this principle to your personal life.

Who is your lighting assistant?
Who is your visual mentor?
Who is your emotional producer?
Who is your critical editor?
Who is the person who gives you energy instead of taking it away?

Many photographers do not sabotage their development because they lack knowledge, but because their surroundings do not fit their vision. They want to think bigger but are surrounded by people who think small. They want to be brave but live in an environment that spreads fear. They want to invest in themselves but surround themselves with people who ridicule everything new. You can be the most talented photographer in the world – if your surroundings constantly drain your energy, your journey will be hard. Too hard.

That is why it is so important to choose your surroundings consciously. Not in the sense of "I'll cut out everyone who annoys me", but in the sense of: I recognise how these people influence me. How they shape me. How they stabilise me – or destabilise me. And I find ways to put myself into the environment that fits my creative future, not my past.

You surely know those moments when you excitedly share an idea and someone says: "Oh, that has never worked." Or: "Nobody makes money with that." Or: "Photography is just a hobby." Sentences like these can be toxic. Not because they are objectively wrong, but because they hold you in a state you actually want to leave behind. Toxic sentences can get stuck in your head and sabotage your perception without you noticing.

But – and now comes the nice part – positive sentences can have the opposite effect. A simple "Do it!" can kick-start an entire career. An "I see that in you" can become a new mindset. An "I believe in you" can be enough for you to dare something you would never have done without that sentence. Sometimes all it takes is one person who believes you can do what you do not yet believe yourself.

The visual language you develop does not come from technique alone. It comes from your life. From conversations. From observations. From culture. From people who inspire or challenge you. When you are with people who have a strong visual world, it rubs off. When you spend time with people who are curious, brave, sensitive, you grow in exactly that direction. The choice of your surroundings decides your photographic taste. Your style. Your attitude. Your aesthetic identity.

And now imagine the opposite case: a photographer with tremendous talent, but nobody around who really recognises this talent. Nobody who offers perspective. Nobody who says: "There is more possible." The probability that this talent withers away is high. Not because the camera is worse. Not because the skill is missing. But because there is no resonance space. Creativity needs an echo.

That is why in next-level development it is so incredibly important to find people who do not just put up with you but really support you. People who see what is at stake. People who understand why some shoots mean so much to you. People who recognise that your development takes time. And people who give you the space you need to grow.

Perhaps you have heard the saying: "Show me your five closest people and I'll tell you how successful you will be." Sounds like a cheap motivational slogan – but it is true. Our surroundings shape our thinking, our energy, our discipline, our possibilities. The five people you spend the most time with are like five filters that determine how clearly you see.

If you surround yourself with people who are brave, you become braver.
If you surround yourself with people who want to grow, you grow with them.
If your surroundings develop, you are automatically pulled along.

But the opposite is just as true. And often imperceptible. Surroundings that do not move hold you back without you really noticing.

Most photographers do not fail because of a lack of talent. They fail because they stay in the wrong environment. Out of habit. Out of loyalty. Out of fear. Or because they never learnt that you are allowed to shape your surroundings consciously. And sometimes you have to.

When you begin a visual journey, take not only your camera with you but also the people who make this journey possible. And choose consciously who comes along. Your surroundings are not responsible for realising your dreams. But they are responsible for not destroying them.

The people behind you are like the tripod in your creative life. You can photograph without a tripod, of course. But you will never have the same stability. Never the same calm. Never the same precision. And certainly not the same freedom to try new things.

If you want to take good pictures, you need strong surroundings. If you want to take extraordinary pictures, you need extraordinary surroundings. And if you want to get into the next-level league, you need people who not only like you but also understand you.

The truth is: surroundings are one of the most underestimated success factors in photography. And nobody talks about it, because it is easier to talk about cameras and lenses. But the real professionals know that the best equipment in the world is useless if the mental conditions are not right. Supportive surroundings are like good light: you only notice them when they are missing.

As a photographer, you will always be the result of your encounters. Of your mentors. Your partners. Your role models. Your friendships. Your clients. Your critics. Your supporters. And of all the people who encouraged you to develop your vision further.

Strong photographers emerge from strong surroundings. And strong surroundings emerge from conscious decisions. Perhaps that is the most important step in your photographic development: having the courage to choose the people around you who fit your future self – not only the one you are today.

It is like with great personalities: behind everyone who is visible stands someone invisible. And in photography these invisible forces are often the real reason why someone is ready to reach the next level. So the question is not: how good is your camera? But: how good are your surroundings?

And sometimes a single person who believes in you is enough for you to make the difference you would never have managed alone.
