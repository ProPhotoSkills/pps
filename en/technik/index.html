Photography is much more than beautiful pictures.

It demands energy, presence and a network that carries you. What you will learn is the insight that photography always begins with you as a person: with your fitness, your focus, your social instinct. You learn how physical presence strengthens your leadership role, how networks become a lifeline in a crisis and how real relationships can shape images. In short: you rethink technique – not just as camera settings or equipment, but as an overall system of body, mind and contacts. Technique here means: consciously using all the tools that enable you to create the image at all.

Frequently asked questions about TECHNIK

Body, mind and network as tools – why technique at ProPhotoSkills begins long before the camera.

What does "technique" mean in the ProPhotoSkills sense – and why not camera technique?
In the ProPhotoSkills system, technique is the overall system of body, mind and contacts – all the tools that enable a photographer to create the image in the first place. Camera settings are the last link. Before them come physical fitness for stamina and presence, mental strength for decisions under pressure, a reliable network for moments of crisis and the attitude with which you lead a set. Whoever only works on the camera is optimising the wrong end of the chain.
Why does jogging help you take better photos?
Jogging is a mental spring clean. After a few minutes of running, the noise in your head gets quieter – the inbox, the stress, the loose ends of the day. Space opens up: for new ideas, for sharper observation, for details you would otherwise overlook. Whoever picks up the camera afterwards sees with different eyes. Photography does not begin with the shutter release – it begins with seeing. And seeing needs a clear head.
What does "you are the director, not just the shutter release" mean in concrete terms on set?
A shoot does not run itself. It is a room full of expectation in which all eyes turn to the photographer. He leads the set, holds the team together, steers the energy. For this he needs clarity, concentration and physical tension – not as a performance, but as real presence. At a fashion shoot in 40-degree heat, the photographer was the only one still moving around the set, handing out water, correcting poses. Whoever is physically weak loses creative control.
How does the photographer's energy transfer to the entire set?
Energy on set is not a metaphor – it is measurable in mood, pace and image quality. When a photographer appears alert, positive and clear, the whole set rises with him. When he seems empty, stressed or tired, everything tips downwards. Especially after the after-lunch slump, when everyone gets tired, the photographer is called upon: ramp up again, motivate, laugh, lead. He is not the quiet artist – he is the host. These reserves come from physical fitness.
Why is fitness a must for photographers – not an optional extra?
Photographers do not work in an air-conditioned office. Their workplace is everywhere: on rooftops, in icy wind, on sand dunes, with heavy equipment under time pressure. Whoever is not fit becomes slower, less attentive and more prone to injury – and thus a burden for the team. At a high-rise shoot in -5-degree wind, the photographer had to keep moving for two hours, photographing and directing at the same time. Fitness is a professional requirement.
What does "attitude = impact" mean in practice?
Your attitude is the first light you set – even before the camera is up. When you enter a set, your walk, your stance, your facial expression immediately create an image. People follow what appears clear and decisive. Physical presence radiates confidence and immediately creates trust – with the team, the model, the client. Sport, running, yoga give you not only fitness but centring: whoever is grounded in their body stays stable under stress too. Your attitude begins in the morning – long before the first click.
What is the connection between mental strength and physical fitness for photographers?
Physical and mental strength depend on each other. Whoever trains regularly builds stress tolerance: they react more calmly under pressure, think more clearly and lose the overview less often. A photographer with strong stamina has reserves – not only for physical work, but for charisma, concentration and decision-making over hours. Concentration comes from physical clarity: whoever breathes calmly thinks clearly.
Why do photographers need a personal network – and not just Instagram followers?
When a location falls through at short notice, a model cancels or a technical problem arises, no Instagram account saves the job. Real contacts do: a friend with a period apartment, a make-up artist who knows a replacement model, a colleague who shares his studio. Photographers have always created their own social network – long before digital platforms existed. This network of trust and mutual help is the invisible foundation that rescues productions or makes them possible in the first place.
What is the "backdrop next door" – and why does it save jobs?
When a booked location was double-booked because of a system error, a WhatsApp message to a designer friend saved the production. Her uncleaned period apartment with herringbone parquet, plants and backlit windows suited the brand's visual language better than the planned minimalist loft. The pictures turned out better than planned. A network is not networking – it is nurturing relationships over years. A good network means: you have a backdrop next door that saves the job when everything goes wrong.
How do you deliberately train the physical and mental side of photography?
It does not take a marathon. It is about consistency: regular exercise that challenges your circulation, clears your head and builds stamina – running, cycling, swimming, yoga. Alongside that: deliberate preparation for long shooting days, running through scenarios in your head, anticipating moments of stress and training your reactions. Physical and mental fitness are not side issues – they are part of the professional craft.
Who is the TECHNIK content at ProPhotoSkills for?
For photographers who notice that they lose control under pressure on set, get exhausted quickly or can no longer lead confidently in difficult situations. And for everyone who wants to understand why some photographers get better on long, tough productions – while others fade. TECHNIK in the ProPhotoSkills sense is self-optimisation as a professional strategy: body, mind and network as trainable tools for sustainably professional work.
