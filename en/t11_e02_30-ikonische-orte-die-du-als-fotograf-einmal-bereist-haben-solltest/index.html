30 iconic places every photographer should visit once

LOCATION, T11_E02

Written by Eric P.

← The client's view - when trust suddenly turns into budget

Introduction

There are places you photograph. And there are places that photograph you.

By this I mean those rare spots that change your eye, stimulate your thinking and stretch your photographic horizon like a rubber band. Places that force you to see differently, react differently, decide differently. Not because they are beautiful – but because they take you further as a photographer. That is the core of progress in the Photoguide: places not as a "bucket list" but as mental training rooms.

Here I take you on a journey to 30 iconic places that every serious photographer should have visited once. Not for Instagram – but for your development, your visual thinking and your photographic self-image.

01

Iceland

the country that teaches you respect for light

Iceland is not a country, it is a lesson.
Light, weather, mood, colours – everything changes by the minute. Here you learn to make quick decisions, to stay flexible in composition and to develop technical confidence that you will need everywhere later.

You will notice how you suddenly start to read clouds.
How you recognise movement before it happens.
How you set the light although you are actually only waiting.

Iceland makes you sharper. And yes – wetter too.

02

New York City

the city that forces you to photograph rhythm

NYC is a permanent beat of movement. As a photographer, here you learn to:

  • control speed

  • translate chaos into lines

  • shape density of people

  • see neon light as a tool, not as a problem

You quickly realise: street photography is not a discipline here but a survival strategy.
If you can photograph New York, you can photograph any big city.

03

Kyoto

the centre of patient photography

Kyoto is light that whispers.
A place that forces calm on you.

Here you learn silence as a subject, negative space as a language and minimalist composition as strength. A geisha portrait you happen to catch in an alley is worth less than a quiet temple room in morning light that you photograph with patience and attitude.

Kyoto teaches you:
"Not everything that is rare is true. But everything true is patient."

04

Sahara Desert

where you understand shadow

The Sahara is photographic brutality.
Contrasts explode, shadows cut like knives, colours look like a filter that isn't one.

Here you learn:

  • to deal with extreme light

  • to compose vastness

  • to tell stories of loneliness or greatness

  • the meaning of lines in nothingness

Sahara photography is the best crash course in form and reduction.

05

Norway's fjords

the school of epic silence

Fjords are not landscapes.
They are spaces that teach you how to photograph depth without exaggerating.

You learn:

  • to use atmospheric layers

  • to read fog

  • compositions with height, depth and reflection

  • long exposure not as an effect but as a language

Norway makes you quieter as a photographer – and stronger.

06

Hong Kong

vertical composition to perfection

Hong Kong is a labyrinth of height, narrowness and light.

Here you learn:

  • to think perspective consistently upwards

  • to shape streams of people

  • to master neon, smoke and chaos

  • to control city light at night

Hong Kong takes your visual pace to a new level. Afterwards you will never again say "too much going on" in a scene.

07

Tuscany

the master class of line landscapes

Tuscany teaches you order in natural forms.
Curves, fields, rows of trees like easels – everything looks painted.

Here you train:

  • leading lines

  • colour theory in a natural environment

  • golden hour as a tool

  • long-term planning of light

Tuscany is not a place for snapshots – it is a place for understanding.

08

Patagonia

wind as the most invisible subject

Patagonia never stops blowing you away.
And that is exactly why it is brilliant.

You learn:

  • to show dynamics in landscapes

  • to use clothing, hair and vegetation as elements of movement

  • to shape contrasts between calm and storm

Patagonia teaches you: not everything has to be controlled – but everything has to be seen.

09

Paris

where timing becomes art

Paris is architecture, romance and people – but above all: timing.

You learn:

  • patience for the "perfect moment"

  • subtle emotions in everyday scenes

  • elegant composition without overloading

Paris is less "wow" and more "ah, okay – this is photography."

10

New Zealand

the master class of diversity

A new biome every hour.

You train:

  • changes of light

  • high-contrast landscapes

  • complexity in natural forms

  • the pace of a travel shoot

New Zealand shapes your visual flexibility.

11

Dubai

the playground of extreme spaces

Here dimensions have escalated.
Buildings, deserts, water – everything monumental.

You learn:

  • to use proportions deliberately

  • to make sterile aesthetics exciting

  • architecture as a story element

  • sunlight in 100 variations

Dubai develops your feeling for scale.

12

Namibia

the place where shadows breathe

The dunes of Sossusvlei are worth a study.

Here you will become a master of:

  • hard lines

  • graphic shapes

  • minimalist power

Namibia shows you how little you need to say a lot.

13

Himalayas

where you photograph with air

At high altitude everything becomes slower – you too.

You learn:

  • visual calm

  • vastness through layering

  • clear focus without distraction

The Himalayas are photographic meditation.

14

Venice

the city of quiet dramas

The light of the lagoon is a chapter in itself.

You train:

  • mood instead of just subject

  • reflections without kitsch

  • composing narrow spaces cleanly

Venice forces you to be sensitive.

15

San Francisco

fog as an art form

Fisherman's hats, Golden Gate, streets – boring?
No. Not if you understand the fog.

You learn:

  • to shape atmospheres

  • depth through haze

  • pastel light

SF is a dream for mood seekers.

16

Bali

colour, spirituality, rhythm

Here you learn:

  • colour deliberately as a language

  • to connect nature & culture

  • ceremonial photography with respect

  • to tame tropical light

Bali is intense – but instructive.

17

Lofoten

the school of Nordic light

Auroras, polar night, soft colours.

You learn:

  • night photography

  • cold & equipment handling

  • colour differentiation in darkness

The Lofoten Islands are a harder, better Iceland.

18

Rio de Janeiro

pure dynamics

Here you learn:

  • lively scenes

  • the courage to get close

  • photographing movement

Rio is a visual firework.

19

Machu Picchu

mysticism & early light

Here you train:

  • morning light

  • mood over colour

  • handling tourism

Magical – but challenging.

20

Grand Canyon

scale, depth, size

The canyon forces you to make decisions:

  • How much sky?

  • How much depth?

  • Which foreground anchor points?

Here you learn to think big.

21

Singapore

order as style

Singapore is clean, bright, strictly ordered – perfect for training:

  • symmetry

  • lines

  • minimal architecture

.

22

Morocco

colours & textures on steroids

Markets, carpets, people, spices.

Here you train:

  • chaotic colour

  • portraits in the flow

  • cultural photography

Morocco is a learning lab.

23

Turkey

a bridge between two visual worlds

You learn:

  • archaic elements

  • modern urban composition

  • historic spaces

Istanbul is a mix you rarely find.

24

Antarctica

white as a challenge

Here you learn:

  • exposure with extreme white

  • abstraction

  • ice as form

Antarctica is a photographic puzzle.

25

Yellowstone

nature as chaos

You train:

  • steam

  • fog

  • unpredictability

Yellowstone is a master class in spontaneous composition.

26

Italian Dolomites

vertical drama

You learn:

  • steep lines

  • sharp rock light

  • mountain scenes with power

One of the best landscape schools in Europe.

27

Bangkok

controlling chaos

Markets, tuk-tuks, night light.

Here you train:

  • speed

  • improvisation

  • neon

Bangkok is pure energy.

28

Provence

pastel colours as if painted

Lavender fields, old walls, warm evening light.

You learn:

  • colour composition

  • tonal harmony

  • naturalness

A place for gentle eyes.

29

Jordan

lines, desert, stone

Petra, Wadi Rum – monuments and vastness.

You train:

  • reddish tones

  • rock architecture

  • shadows

Jordan is epic, but technically demanding.

30

Canadian Rockies

nature with punch

Here you learn:

  • strong colours

  • crystal-clear lakes

  • hard changes of light

The Rockies are the gym of nature photography.

Conclusion: why these places change you

Because photography is not just technique.
Not just talent.
Not just gear.

But experience.

The places above are not pretty postcards.
They are spaces that force you to see better, work more consciously and redefine yourself as a photographer.

Each of these places sharpens a different part of your abilities:

  • light

  • mood

  • colour

  • speed

  • patience

  • reflection

  • storytelling

  • technical control

  • visual thinking

  • decision-making

Progress does not happen in the studio.
Progress happens on the road – where you are forced to react to light, weather, people and culture.

When you have photographed these 30 places, you will notice something:

You see differently.
You think differently.
You work differently.

And you are closer to your "next level" than ever before.
