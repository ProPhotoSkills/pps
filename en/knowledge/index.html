Frequently asked questions about KNOWLEDGE

What really decides before the shutter is pressed for the first time – from preparation and team dynamics to da Vinci, chiaroscuro and emotional expression.

What does the KNOWLEDGE section at ProPhotoSkills cover?
KNOWLEDGE covers the foundation of professional photography – everything that is decided before the first click. Topics include preparation as a core competence, general knowledge as a tool on set, team dynamics, historical image principles from da Vinci to chiaroscuro, composition, anatomy, optics, creative leadership with agency teams and emotional expression beyond technical perfection. The principle: your mind is your most important lens.
What does "70 % of the photo job happens before the click" mean in concrete terms?
Most professional image work happens invisibly: in concept development, the client briefing, light planning, team coordination, location research and mental preparation. Anyone who starts thinking on set has already lost – time, energy and often image quality. 70 % of the result is decided before the camera is in your hand. ProPhotoSkills shows systematically what really happens in this phase and how to shape it actively.
What is the "invisible before the click" – and why is it decisive?
The invisible is everything that no client sees but that shapes every image: the calm with which you arrive on set, the clarity of the concept, the quality of the team, the prepared lighting idea. Many think the magic happens during the shoot – in fact, the shoot only decides whether the preparation worked. Whoever masters the invisible works more calmly, faster and consistently produces at a high level.
Why is a well-rehearsed team more important than expensive equipment?
A camera creates no atmosphere. A light shaper builds no flow. A lens does not calm a nervous model. People do that – assistants, stylists, hair & make-up artists, client service. A team that knows and trusts each other solves problems quickly, communicates without words and creates the energy that makes strong images possible in the first place. Nurturing working relationships is therefore the most important investment a professional photographer can make.
Why do photographers need a good general education?
On a professional set you work with agencies, art directors, clients and models – people from the most diverse worlds. Anyone who can talk confidently about culture, history, design or current topics builds trust, breaks the ice and is perceived as a creative partner – not as an image supplier. General knowledge is not a hobby: it is an invisible tool for leading the set and building relationships.
How do you ignite creativity on set with an agency team?
The mood on set is no coincidence – it is a leadership task. During set changes and waiting phases, when everyone is staring at their phone, a targeted impulse is needed: an unexpected question, a short creative game, a thought that pulls people out of everyday mode. The photographer decides whether the set falls into lethargy or tips into energy. General knowledge, humour and conversation skills are the tools for this – not the camera.
What does Leonardo da Vinci have to do with modern photography?
Da Vinci's principles can be transferred directly to photography: chiaroscuro as a lighting principle, knowledge of anatomy for directing models, laws of composition such as the golden ratio, and the multidisciplinary view beyond the boundaries of one's field. Whoever understands why the old masters worked the way they did has internalised principles that are timeless – and makes more conscious decisions on set than someone who only knows camera settings.
What is chiaroscuro and how do I apply it in photography?
Chiaroscuro is the technique of deliberate light-dark contrast – known from the paintings of Caravaggio and da Vinci. In photography it creates depth, drama and visual focus. Instead of lighting everything evenly, you deliberately use shadows to model volume and guide the eye. Whoever masters chiaroscuro works with light as a means of expression – not as a source of illumination.
Why is knowledge of anatomy important when working with models?
Whoever understands how bodies are built – joint axes, muscles, weight distribution – can direct models more precisely and more respectfully. Instead of describing a pose, you explain a movement. Instead of correcting, you create a body awareness that leads to more natural, stronger images. Knowledge of anatomy from art history is a direct craft tool – not an academic add-on.
What is the Mona Lisa effect – and what does it mean for portrait photography?
Technically perfect images that feel emotionally empty: that is the opposite of the Mona Lisa effect. Da Vinci's masterpiece fascinates centuries later because it carries an expression that leaves room for interpretation. Portrait photography that really hits home looks for exactly this moment: not the perfect pose, but the genuine expression. Every fully choreographed shot that closes this space loses impact – no matter how technically correct it is.
What does a multidisciplinary approach mean in modern photography?
The best images are not created because everything was correctly exposed – but because the photographer sees connections that others do not see. Whoever combines art, psychology, communication, cultural history and visual principles thinks in a different dimension. Modern advertising photography is not a purely technical craft – it is a creative leadership task that demands that photographers think beyond the boundaries of their field. Curiosity is the decisive driving force.
